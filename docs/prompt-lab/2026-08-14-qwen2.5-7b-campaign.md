# Prompt Lab campaign — `qwen2.5-7b-instruct-1m`

Date: 2026-08-14
Benchmark: same as the gemma-4-12b and qwen3.5-9b campaigns —
`tools/prompt-lab/data/positions.json` (seed 42; screen = first 150
positions, final = first 600), one request per position, `temperature 0`,
no correction retries.

**No `reasoning_effort` this time.** Unlike gemma-4-12b and qwen3.5-9b,
qwen2.5-7b-instruct is not a reasoning model — verified live before the
run: a plain chat request returns `content: "e5"` with
`reasoning_content: ""`. The flag was therefore not passed, and the
adapter does not carry it.

## Verdict

**Race winner: `v7-cot-legal`. Adapter ships `v1-legal-list` instead** —
see "Why the runner-up ships" below.

| variant (final, n=600) | match    | ±se  | match+legal | illegal | mean ms |
| ---------------------- | -------- | ---- | ----------- | ------- | ------- |
| **v7-cot-legal**       | **6.3%** | 1.0% | 93.3%       | 40      | 2482    |
| v1-legal-list          | 5.2%     | 0.9% | **95.5%**   | 27      | 887     |
| v8-karpov-legal        | 4.3%     | 0.8% | 91.0%       | 54      | 891     |
| v0-baseline (control)  | 1.5%     | 0.5% | 21.2%       | 473     | 692     |

Against the production control at equal n: **match 6.3% vs 1.5%**
(4.8 pp, ≈4.3σ) and legality **93.3% vs 21.2%**. The baseline here is
even worse than it was on qwen3.5 (21% vs 33% legal) — nearly four of
five replies are illegal, so in today's app this model plays essentially
random chess through the fallback path. An adapter is unambiguously
justified.

## Screen (n=150), all nine variants

v7-cot-legal 7.3%/92.7%, v1-legal-list 5.3%/**97.3%**,
v8-karpov-legal 5.3%/92.7%, v6-pgn-completion 2.7%/53.3%,
v0-baseline 2.0%/22.7%, v5-fewshot 1.3%/17.3%, v3-board 0.7%/23.3%,
v4-cot 0.0%/19.3% (3.4 s), v2-uci 0.0%/5.3% (98 unparseable).

How this model differs from the two already profiled:

- **PGN continuation collapses.** `v6-pgn-completion`, the outright
  winner on qwen3.5-9b (10.7% match / 74.8% legal), scores 2.7% / 53.3%
  here. Same vendor prefix, opposite optimum — an instruct model without
  the reasoning stack does not continue a game the way qwen3.5 does.
- **Only the legal-move list works.** All three finalists are legal-list
  variants; nothing else clears 25% legality. This mirrors gemma-4 rather
  than qwen3.5.
- **Weakest of the three models in absolute terms**: 6.3% best-variant
  match vs 10.7% (qwen3.5-9b) and 9.8% (gemma-4-12b).
- `v2-uci` is unusable — 98 of 150 replies unparseable, the model does not
  produce coordinate notation on request.

## Why the runner-up ships

`v7-cot-legal` wins the race by rule (highest match), but the gap does
not survive scrutiny:

- **Match difference is inside noise.** 6.3% ± 1.0% vs 5.2% ± 0.9% — the
  1.1 pp gap is smaller than the standard error of the difference
  (≈1.3 pp), i.e. under 1σ. The race's ranking rule has no tie-break for
  this; the campaign does.
- **v7 is 2.8× slower** (2482 ms vs 887 ms per move). With the app's real
  10:00-per-side clock — and Black's clock ticking while the model thinks
  (`useChessClock`) — that is a material cost paid for an unmeasurable
  gain.
- **v7 is also _less_ legal** (93.3% vs 95.5%; 40 illegal vs 27). Every
  illegal move costs a correction retry, which is another full request —
  so v7's per-move latency is worse than the mean suggests.

Cheaper, more legal, statistically indistinguishable in strength:
`v1-legal-list` is the better production prompt. This is the same call
gemma4.ts already made in spirit (it ships v1 too).

## Failure flavor

**Baseline (no legal list) hallucinates pieces that are gone.** Position
`r1b4r/pp1nkppp/2p1p3/3p4/2PP4/N5P1/PP1KPPBP/R2R4 b - - 2 13`, reached
after `…Qxd2+ Kxd2` — queens are off the board. The model replies
`Qd6`. That is the archetype of the 473 illegal baseline moves: it is
not misjudging the position, it is not reading it.

**With the legal list, the residual 4.5% illegal replies are token
echoes, not blunders.** Two of the 27:

- `r4rk1/1p1b2bp/1q3Bp1/pPn2p2/8/PP3NP1/3QPPBP/2R1R1K1 b` — reply `Qb6`,
  which is where the queen already stands: a null move, copied out of the
  position rather than the list.
- `8/2R2b2/5pk1/4nNp1/4K1P1/5P2/8/8 b` — reply `Nxd5`, a capture of an
  empty square.

The legal list does not make the model _understand_ the position; it
constrains it to a menu, and the misses that remain are the model
drifting off that menu into nearby-looking tokens.

## Adapter (`src/llm/adapters/qwen25.ts`)

Single-stage, structurally the same as `gemma4.ts` minus the reasoning
flag:

- `matches: (id) => id.includes('qwen2.5')` — disjoint from
  `qwen35Adapter`'s `includes('qwen3.5')`, so the two never contend.
- v1-legal-list chat prompt, carrying the app's ELO persona (a real
  product feature) in place of the campaign's generic grandmaster line —
  the same substitution gemma4.ts made, on the same evidence that persona
  wording moves the result only within noise (v1 5.3% vs v8-karpov 5.3%
  at screen n=150, identical here).
- Correction sentence appended on retries; `parseSanCandidates` for the
  reply.
- `sampling: { temperature: 0, maxTokens: 64 }` — **no** `reasoningEffort`.

Expected production behavior: ~95% of moves legal on the first attempt,
most of the rest recovered by the correction retry, random fallback only
in the residue — versus ~79% of moves falling to correction-or-random
today.

## Not recommended in the UI

This model is **not** added to `recommendedModels.json`. It works with
the adapter, but at 6.3% best-variant match it is the weakest of the
three profiled models, and the star badge is for models we would steer a
user toward.
